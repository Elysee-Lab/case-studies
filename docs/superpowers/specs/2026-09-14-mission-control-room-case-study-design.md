## 1. Purpose

Build a public portfolio case study that tells the full story behind Mission Control Room as a business transformation programme, not merely as an AI lead-scoring application.

The case study must demonstrate the progression from a neglected and noisy inquiry channel to a measurable inquiry-to-revenue operating model, and then to an independent AI-enabled decision layer that could be tested safely alongside existing enterprise systems.

The page must simultaneously show:

1. Eliza Bingul's business transformation and change-management capability.
2. Revenue Operations, attribution and measurement capability.
3. Product and AI system design capability.
4. Hands-on technical judgement and implementation capability.
5. Human-in-the-loop governance and responsible AI design.
6. The ability to work in a complex matrix organisation with fragmented ownership and legacy constraints.
7. A repeatable transformation method that is portable beyond the original real-estate context.

The public case study is a portfolio artifact. It must never expose information that would allow a third party to reconstruct, access or infer confidential enterprise systems, data, credentials, private decision rules or individual records.

## 2. Core thesis

Mission Control Room did not begin as an AI project.

It began years earlier with a business question:

> Are valuable commercial opportunities being lost because they are hidden inside inquiry noise and fragmented processes?

The transformation evolved through several stages:

1. recognise the hidden value in a noisy inquiry channel;
2. manually identify and protect high-value inquiries;
3. establish a regional Lead Qualification capability and operating team;
4. define ownership, qualification, routing, follow-up and SLA principles;
5. measure inquiry quality and business outcomes;
6. prove commercially meaningful outcomes and make attribution visible;
7. change marketing and stakeholder behaviour around measurable business contribution;
8. automate repetitive qualification and feedback processes;
9. identify the structural limits created by fragmented systems, documentation and ownership;
10. design Mission Control Room as a parallel intelligence and experimentation layer;
11. use AI only after the underlying process, data, decision logic and governance were understood.

The key message is:

> AI was the accelerator, not the starting point.

## 5. Narrative structure

### Section 1 — The problem existed long before AI

Set the enterprise context without criticising the organisation.

Public-safe framing:

- large multinational, multi-market environment;
- Salesforce and Salesforce Marketing Cloud were part of the existing commercial and marketing stack;
- additional operational knowledge and evidence lived across tools such as SharePoint, dashboards, spreadsheets and local processes;
- ownership was distributed across multiple stakeholder groups;
- documentation and process knowledge were uneven;
- even relatively small changes could require several teams, dependencies and release cycles.

Key insight:

**The problem was not lack of technology. It was the gap between systems, ownership, decisions and outcomes.**

### Section 2 — Noise was hiding commercial value

Explain that high volumes of weak, irrelevant or incorrectly routed inquiries made the overall channel easy to dismiss.

Introduce the principle:

**Noise does not mean no value.**

The strategic shift was to stop judging the channel by average quality and instead identify commercially meaningful signals hidden within the total volume.

### Section 3 — Before automation: the human intelligence layer

Show the earliest operating model:

`manual review → identification of valuable inquiries → nudging → escalation → follow-up → evidence`

Purpose:

- make sure potentially important opportunities did not disappear;
- learn what signals distinguished valuable inquiries from noise;
- identify failure points in routing and ownership;
- create evidence before requesting larger technical change.

This is the human prototype of what later became an automated qualification layer.

### Section 4 — Building the regional Lead Qualification capability

Position this as an initiated and led capability, not participation in an existing programme.

Show the evolution:

- regional lead qualification team established;
- collaboration extended through a matrix model to other stakeholder groups;
- responsibilities and ownership clarified;
- qualification and routing processes formalised;
- feedback and follow-up were introduced;
- roles were later restructured as the capability matured;
- repetitive parts of the process were progressively automated.

Key leadership message:

**When the problem had no natural owner, a new operating capability was created around it.**

### Section 5 — Measurement changed the conversation

Explain the introduction of Power BI reporting and the move from volume reporting to quality and outcome measurement.

Public-safe dimensions may include:

- geography;
- service line;
- business line / team;
- lead quality;
- response / handling state;
- conversion / outcome where evidence existed.

Public-safe business evidence:

- early executive case studies linked marketing-generated inquiries to approximately USD 1 million / seven-figure commercial outcomes;
- additional deals existed beyond the headline examples;
- this evidence helped demonstrate that the channel could generate material business value.

Preferred external wording where confidentiality is a concern:

**"Executive case studies connected marketing-generated inquiries to seven-figure commercial outcomes."**

Do not publish client names, deal names, opportunity IDs, exact commissions, internal revenue splits or confidential contract details.

### Section 6 — Attribution changed marketing behaviour

Show the organisational shift:

`activity → outcomes`

`campaign metrics → business impact`

`lead generation → inquiry-to-revenue`

`reporting → attribution`

The point is not that one dashboard changed the organisation overnight. The point is that credible commercial evidence made inquiry quality, follow-up and attribution relevant to stakeholders who had previously not prioritised the topic.

Core line:

**Marketing attribution stopped being only a reporting exercise and became part of the operating model.**

### Section 7 — The scaling problem

Once the channel was treated as meaningful, new questions appeared:

- Who qualifies an inquiry?
- Who owns the next action?
- What is high priority?
- When does SLA start?
- What is a genuine commercial inquiry versus vendor, recruitment, test or spam traffic?
- How should an existing client be treated?
- How are cross-border inquiries handled?
- Which service line or team should receive the inquiry?
- How is follow-up recorded?
- How is a later opportunity linked back to the original inquiry?
- Which system is authoritative for which part of the process?

This shows the transition from a marketing problem to a combined:

**process + data + technology + ownership + behaviour + governance problem.**

### Section 8 — Change management: reduce noise, shorten SLA

Visualise the operating-model change.

Before:

`all inquiries → manual attention → noise → inconsistent response → unclear ownership → uncertain attribution`

Target:

`inquiry → triage → valuable signal surfaced → owner → SLA → follow-up → opportunity → attribution`

Primary outcome:

**Reduce cognitive and operational noise so people can respond faster to opportunities that matter.**

This section should make clear that automation was designed to improve human attention, not simply remove humans.

### Section 9 — Why Mission Control Room was built outside the core systems

This is a central transformation principle.

Public-safe description:

The existing enterprise platforms were powerful but interconnected. Changes often depended on multiple owners, documentation, release cycles and existing automation. A separate intelligence layer enabled experimentation without destabilising production workflows.

Mission Control Room was therefore designed as a:

**parallel intelligence, decision-support and experimentation layer.**

It could:

- ingest copies of relevant inquiry data;
- apply qualification and routing logic outside the core stack;
- be changed quickly;
- support controlled testing;
- collect structured human feedback;
- measure outcomes;
- generate concrete requirements for future changes to enterprise systems.

It was explicitly not intended to become an uncontrolled shadow CRM.

### Section 10 — Optionality: the learning remained valuable even if the final system changed

Explain the workaround/test-harness philosophy.

Even if the organisation later rebuilt the capability in Salesforce, another enterprise platform or a new architecture, the experiment would still produce reusable assets:

- process maps;
- field and signal requirements;
- qualification taxonomy;
- ownership rules;
- routing logic;
- edge cases;
- human override patterns;
- SLA requirements;
- attribution requirements;
- conversion definitions;
- tested UX needs;
- integration requirements;
- governance lessons.

This demonstrates transformation optionality rather than attachment to one tool.

### Section 12 — AI architecture

Public diagram:

`Inquiry → Preparation → Research → Interpretation → Decision Support → Orchestration → Human Review → Approved Output`

Describe specialist roles generically. Agent codenames may be used as product flavour only if no confidential prompt or exact rule is exposed.

Explain that the architecture separates concerns rather than asking a single model to perform every task.

Do not expose:

- full prompts;
- exact scoring weights;
- thresholds;
- private reference datasets;
- internal IDs;
- credentials;
- model/provider secrets;
- detailed security topology.

### Section 13 — Human Control Room

Make this a visually prominent section.

A reviewer should be able to understand:

- what the system inferred;
- which evidence or signals contributed;
- confidence;
- missing information;
- discrepancies;
- recommended action;
- suggested ownership;
- prior context;
- whether a human previously corrected the system;
- outcome / conversion evidence later in the lifecycle.

Human controls:

- approve;
- override;
- add context;
- link related records;
- record or validate conversion.

Core principle:

**AI prepares and recommends. Humans retain authority over consequential decisions.**

### Section 14 — System-agnostic integration

Position Mission Control as deliberately easy to connect rather than dependent on one CRM vendor.

Public architecture:

`CRM / marketing automation / website / form / spreadsheet / API`

→ **standardised ingestion + configurable field mapping**

→ **Mission Control Room**

→ `CRM / BI / data warehouse / marketing automation / workflow system`

Supported integration concepts to describe publicly:

- JSON / API;
- webhooks / POST;
- CSV / batch exchange;
- configurable mappings;
- BI-friendly exports or read models;
- modular downstream destinations.

Do not expose real endpoints, secret URLs, auth headers or internal object IDs.

### Section 15 — Closing attribution gaps across fragmented systems

Explain the business problem:

- source can live in one system;
- qualification in another;
- human follow-up may happen outside the original marketing platform;
- opportunity can be created later in CRM;
- outcome knowledge can exist only with local teams.

Mission Control's role is to create a traceable evidence chain:

`Inquiry → Qualification → Owner → Follow-up → Opportunity → Outcome → Revenue evidence`

The system does not need to replace the CRM to improve attribution. It can link evidence and identify missing links.

### Section 16 — Behavioural design: the carrot method

Show that process design is also behavioural design.

Instead of treating conversion reporting as another administrative burden, the design included mechanisms that make contribution visible.

Public-safe examples:

- conversion feedback mechanism;
- attribution leaderboard / recognition;
- visibility of successful outcomes;
- making reporting useful to business teams rather than useful only to marketing analysts.

Core line:

**Not every data gap is solved by another API. Sometimes the missing integration is human behaviour.**

### Section 17 — Business outcomes

Do not end with technical architecture.

Primary business outcomes / intended operating outcomes:

- fewer valuable inquiries missed;
- shorter qualification and routing SLA;
- reduced manual noise;
- clearer ownership;
- more consistent qualification;
- better prioritisation of human attention;
- business outcomes linked back to source;
- marketing contribution made visible;
- reusable business logic independent of one platform;
- concrete evidence for future core-system redesign;
- faster experimentation without destabilising core systems.

Distinguish clearly between:

- demonstrated historical outcomes;
- capabilities already implemented in Mission Control;
- future / planned enterprise integration outcomes.

Never convert intended benefits into claimed realised metrics without evidence.

### Section 18 — What I learned

Use candid portfolio-quality learnings:

1. Lead qualification is an operating-model problem before it is an AI problem.
2. Noise can hide high-value opportunities; average lead quality is a poor measure of channel value.
3. Attribution must be designed into the process, not added after the fact.
4. Automation cannot repair ambiguous ownership.
5. Human review needs reasoning, confidence and context, not just a score.
6. Historical outcomes are useful only when definitions are consistent.
7. External experimentation layers can accelerate learning when core systems cannot change quickly.
8. The output of an experiment is not only software; it is validated process knowledge.
9. AI should not own decisions that the organisation itself has not yet defined clearly.
10. Behavioural incentives can close data gaps that technical integrations cannot.

- executive case studies linked inquiries to seven-figure commercial outcomes;
- inquiry-to-revenue architecture and qualification processes were developed;

### Implemented / testable

- multi-agent qualification pipeline;
- configurable mappings;
- human review / override;
- feedback loops;
- conversion feedback;
- export mechanisms;
- audit and configuration.

### Planned / future integration

Do not describe as realised:

- full live bidirectional CRM integration if not completed;
- fully closed-loop global revenue attribution if still dependent on enterprise integration;
- organisation-wide rollout or adoption where not evidenced;
- production auto-approval at scale unless verified.
