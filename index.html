What I built first

First I built the system. Then I started scaling it.

This is not a case of optimising a business engine that was already running. I first defined the brand strategy and positioning, business model and commercialisation paths, audience-specific value propositions, offer architecture, go-to-market and acquisition system, end-to-end journeys, digital platform, clinical knowledge spine, operating standards and measurement layer. Only once that foundation existed did channel optimisation and scaling become the main question.

Venture-building scope: I treated DietoLab as one connected business system, not a collection of marketing initiatives. Brand, offer, revenue model, customer journeys, technology, clinical quality, sales and measurement were designed to reinforce one another.
1

Brand strategy & positioning

I defined DietoLab's positioning: what the brand would stand for, which problems it could credibly solve, and how specialist nutrition care could combine clinical quality, accessibility and technology without becoming a generic marketplace.

2

Business model & commercialisation

I designed several complementary revenue paths rather than relying on one consultation model: B2C care, employer and institutional services, Health Days, research-led offers, value-added services and a partner network that can add local capacity over time.

3

Value proposition & service design

I translated different audience needs into distinct propositions for patients, dietitians, companies and institutions, then shaped packages, supporting services and value-added elements around the outcome each group actually needs.

4

Full-funnel GTM & acquisition

I designed acquisition across organic search, local SEO, content, paid media, social, newsletter and owned channels, partnerships, referrals, B2B outbound, institutional outreach and dietitian recruitment, with different entry points for different audiences.

5

End-to-end journeys & lifecycle

I mapped the full relationship from first contact through qualification, booking or offer, delivery, payment, follow-up, retention and expansion. These journeys became the blueprint for CRM states, automation and operational ownership.

6

Product platform & toolset

I built the digital operating layer: public acquisition surfaces, patient and dietitian panels, booking, CRM, sales tooling, research workflows, Health Days, payments, documentation, automation and analytics needed to run the model.

7

Clinical Brain / Single Spine & AI

I designed a shared knowledge and decision spine so clinical and AI-enabled tools use consistent sources, rules and quality controls rather than becoming isolated features. The aim is better organised work and more consistent patient support, with professional judgement retained.

8

Operating model, quality & measurement

I defined processes, standards, roles, documentation, auditability and measurement so the business could learn from real behaviour. Growth analytics and attribution were therefore part of the operating system, not a reporting layer added at the end.

What this case study shows

It brings together the strategy, actions, product decisions, measurable outcomes, failed assumptions and remaining gaps. The goal is not to present a polished success story, but to show how a real growth system was built and refined while the business was still small.

Why show it this way?

Because the most transferable capability is not one SEO result or one feature. It is the ability to connect business model, customer journey, technology, sales, operations and evidence into one system, identify the actual bottleneck, and change priorities when the data contradicts the original assumption.

After the foundations were built

After building the foundations: I measured, learned and rebuilt.

The brand, offer, journeys and first product system came first. This section shows what happened next: how evidence changed hypotheses, priorities and the way individual parts of the system were improved.

01 · Baseline after buildGrowth analytics

After the first system version: establish a baseline

Measurement was not the beginning of DietoLab. It was the first optimisation layer after the initial brand, offer, journeys and platform had been designed: GA4, GTM, Clarity, SEO metadata and a reliable point of reference.

Learning: once a system exists, the next job is to make it observable. Without a trustworthy baseline, optimisation creates activity, not knowledge.
02 · SEO architectureProduct + SEO

Expand the search surface, then improve its quality

Specialisation, city and expert architecture created more discoverable entry points. Later work focused on canonicalisation, SSR and per-page quality rather than simply adding pages.

Learning: scale in SEO comes from useful architecture and real supply, not from programmatic page volume alone.
03 · CausalityEvidence-first

Do not give the latest deployment all the credit

The August visibility break started before full SSR rollout. The report therefore treats SSR as supportive, not as the single cause.

Learning: sequence is not causality. Change-point timing matters.
04 · AttributionData engineering

Separate broken systems from low sample size

The first audit made parts of the funnel look broken. Cohort analysis showed that some stages simply had no eligible post-deployment cases, while cross-device stitching and the consent queue were real defects.

Learning: the denominator matters as much as the numerator.
05 · Data hygieneGovernance

Remove internal behaviour from growth claims

Known admin-linked touchpoints were removed from external-facing acquisition counts and historical GA4 was relabelled as directional where retrospective staff exclusion is imperfect.

Learning: credibility improves when weak evidence is downgraded rather than defended.
06 · Business modelStrategy

The growth bottleneck became supply

Once visibility, tooling and pipelines existed, the strategic problem changed: one active public dietitian cannot monetise or fulfil a multi-city demand engine.

Learning: the data exposed a structural constraint: demand growth without specialist capacity simply creates the next bottleneck.

Blocker A - cross-device stitching · fixed and validated

The fix is additive and keeps the existing model. The admin-linked production validation journey from 10 September was resolved successfully; it is not counted as external patient acquisition.

  • the anon ID from signup is stored in account metadata,
  • after first login, stitching covers both the signup ID and current browser ID,
  • pending attribution conversions are re-resolved after touchpoints are attached,
  • the admin-linked validation journey now has 8 pre-signup touchpoints and `pending = false`; a controlled external same-device / second-device journey remains a regression test.

Blocker B - server-side destination queue · fixed, E2E test next

The historical 44 events expired and are intentionally not replayed. I fixed the queue logic; the remaining evidence gap is a controlled full-consent event that proves the complete server-side delivery path.

  • `consent_denied` still ends the event permanently as fail-closed,
  • `consent_unavailable` waits and retries every 15 minutes for up to 72 hours,
  • the consent snapshot is stored at enqueue,
  • the invalid delivered status write was also fixed; the panel shows generated / delivered / queued / waiting for consent / denied / expired / errors separately for GA4 and Meta.

Gap C - LinkedIn click ID · fixed and tested

Capture v4 records `li_fat_id` alongside Google, Meta and Bing identifiers, only when ad consent is granted.

  • new `li_fat_id` column in `attribution_touchpoints`,
  • UUID format validation and parameter parking before consent,
  • deduplication and fallback to older RPC versions,
  • build OK and attribution tests 56/56.

Definition of a closed funnel for DietoLab

B2C: source → landing → subscriber/profile → self-booking → payment → retention. B2B dietitian: source → application → interview → offer → onboarding → active partner. B2B company: source → organisation/contact → qualification → survey/offer → won → delivery. I designed CPL, CAC and ROAS as path-specific metrics because one blended number would hide the differences between B2C, partner supply and B2B demand.

Join the network

More than brand and marketing. DietoLab is designed as shared infrastructure for running and growing a practice.

The partnership is built from several complementary layers. The dietitian remains the professional responsible for patient care, while DietoLab provides infrastructure that would otherwise require multiple tools, skills and operating systems to build independently.

Next horizon

The next hypotheses I chose to validate

I do not end the case study with a generic marketing backlog. The evidence points to four specific hypotheses, each with its own validation criterion.

1

E2E validation after the fixes

After fixing the two confirmed defects, I defined a small regression set: same-device and second-device signup, one self-booking, and one full-consent server-side event. The point is to validate the complete path without mistaking a technical test for a business outcome.

Validation criterion: first/converting touch remains correct for new signups, the LinkedIn click identifier is captured for eligible Ads traffic, and the server-side queue delivers a valid full-consent event.
2

B2C: convert visibility into self-booking

For B2C I prioritised high-exposure pages: improving CTR, making CTAs more consistent, tagging social/newsletter links and keeping one clear content → trust → self-booking path.

Validation criterion: qualified self-booking rate grows and attribution covers the full journey without using manual bookings as a substitute.
3

B2B dietitians: prove partner activation economics

For the partner network I designed three contrasting pilots and an end-to-end measurement path: offer → onboarding → profile live → first booking → repeat care → B2B contribution. This is how I intend to replace scenario assumptions with real partner-cohort medians.

Validation criterion: median time-to-live, days-to-first-booking, 90-day new patients, utilisation and repeat-care rate become reportable.
4

B2B companies: product-led pipeline

For B2B I defined the next hypothesis around one consistent company journey: lead → qualification → survey / Health Day / offer → won → delivery, with research and Health Days measured as distinct entry products into a longer commercial relationship.

Validation criterion: stage-to-stage conversion and velocity are visible by source and product, and the survey produces both business insight and a clear next sales step.

Problem A - łączenie historii między urządzeniami · naprawiony i sprawdzony

Naprawa rozszerza dotychczasowy mechanizm, nie zmieniając jego podstaw. Przypadek produkcyjny z 10 września, powiązany z kontem administratora, został poprawnie rozliczony. Służy jako techniczne potwierdzenie działania i nie jest liczony jako pozyskanie zewnętrznego pacjenta.

  • anonimowy identyfikator z chwili rejestracji jest zapisywany w metadanych konta,
  • po pierwszym logowaniu mechanizm łączy identyfikator z rejestracji z identyfikatorem bieżącej przeglądarki,
  • oczekujące konwersje atrybucyjne są ponownie rozliczane po połączeniu punktów kontaktu,
  • przypadek walidacyjny powiązany z administratorem ma już 8 punktów kontaktu sprzed rejestracji i `pending = false`; kontrolowany test na tym samym i drugim urządzeniu pozostaje testem regresji.

Problem B - kolejka wysyłek po stronie serwera · naprawiona, pozostał test całej ścieżki

Historyczne 44 zdarzenia wygasły i świadomie nie są wysyłane ponownie. Naprawiłam logikę kolejki. Pozostałym dowodem do zebrania jest kontrolowany przypadek z pełną zgodą użytkownika, który potwierdzi dostarczenie całej ścieżki po stronie serwera.

  • `consent_denied` nadal trwale kończy zdarzenie bez wysyłki, zgodnie z zasadą domyślnego braku zgody,
  • `consent_unavailable` pozostawia zdarzenie w oczekiwaniu i ponawia próbę co 15 minut, maksymalnie przez 72 godziny,
  • stan zgody jest zapisywany w chwili dodania zdarzenia do kolejki,
  • naprawiono również zapis statusu dostarczonych zdarzeń; panel pokazuje osobno dla GA4 i Meta liczbę zdarzeń utworzonych, dostarczonych, oczekujących, czekających na zgodę, odrzuconych z powodu braku zgody, wygasłych i błędnych.

Problem C - identyfikator kliknięcia LinkedIn · naprawiony i przetestowany

Wersja v4 zapisuje `li_fat_id` obok identyfikatorów Google, Meta i Bing, wyłącznie po uzyskaniu zgody reklamowej.

  • nowa kolumna `li_fat_id` w `attribution_punkty kontaktu`,
  • walidacja formatu UUID i parkowanie parametru przed zgodą,
  • usuwanie duplikatów i możliwość skorzystania ze starszej wersji RPC,
  • kompilacja przebiega poprawnie, a wszystkie 56 testów atrybucji przechodzi.

Jakie hipotezy wybrałam do kolejnej walidacji

Nie kończę case study listą ogólnych „dobrych praktyk”. Z danych wynikają cztery konkretne hipotezy, które wybrałam do dalszej walidacji, każda z własnym kryterium wyniku.

1

Walidacja E2E po naprawach

Po naprawieniu dwóch potwierdzonych defektów zdefiniowałam mały zestaw testów regresyjnych: rejestrację na tym samym i drugim urządzeniu, samodzielną rezerwację oraz zdarzenie po stronie serwera przy pełnej zgodzie. Ich zadaniem jest sprawdzenie pełnej ścieżki bez mylenia testu technicznego z wynikiem biznesowym.

Kryterium walidacji: poprawne zachowanie first/converting touch w nowych rejestracjach, zapis identyfikatora kliknięcia dla LinkedIn Ads i dostarczenie prawidłowego zdarzenia po stronie serwera przy pełnej zgodzie.
2

B2C: zamieniać widoczność w samodzielne rezerwacje

Dla B2C priorytet ustawiłam na stronach z dużą liczbą wyświetleń: poprawie CTR, spójniejszych wezwaniach do działania, oznaczaniu linków z social i newslettera oraz jednej jasnej ścieżce treść → zaufanie → rezerwacja.

Kryterium walidacji: wzrost odsetka kwalifikowanych samodzielnych rezerwacji oraz pełna atrybucja ścieżki bez traktowania ręcznie umawianych wizyt jako zamiennika.
3

B2B dietetycy: udowodnić ekonomikę aktywacji partnera

Dla sieci partnerskiej zaprojektowałam trzy kontrastujące pilotaże i pełny pomiar procesu: oferta → wdrożenie → uruchomiony profil → pierwsza rezerwacja → kolejne wizyty → udział zleceń B2B. Chcę w ten sposób zastąpić scenariusze rzeczywistymi medianami kolejnych kohort partnerów.

Kryterium walidacji: raportowalne mediany czasu do uruchomienia, czasu do pierwszej rezerwacji, liczby nowych pacjentów w 90 dni, wykorzystania dostępnego czasu i odsetka pacjentów wracających.
4

B2B firmy: uporządkowany proces sprzedaży

Dla B2B kolejną hipotezę zdefiniowałam wokół jednej spójnej ścieżki: kontakt → kwalifikacja → badanie / Dzień Zdrowia / oferta → wygrana → realizacja. Badania i Dni Zdrowia traktuję jako odrębne produkty wejściowe, które mogą prowadzić do dłuższej współpracy.

Kryterium walidacji: widoczna konwersja i tempo przechodzenia między etapami według źródła i produktu oraz potwierdzenie, że badanie ankietowe generuje zarówno insight, jak i kolejny krok sprzedażowy.
